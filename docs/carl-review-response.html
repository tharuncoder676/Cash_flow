01

Your findings, and what we did

7 observations

Each item below was traced to its cause in the code rather than patched at the surface, so the same fault cannot return by a different route.

R1

The stated time commitment was wrong

Fixed

You were right that the figures did not add up. The site now states the total in one place and every other mention reads from it: about two and a half hours a week — roughly 90 minutes of lessons plus one 60–75 minute live session. There is no longer anywhere that quotes 90 minutes on its own.

R2

Question 3 of the diagnostic scored backwards

Fixed

You were right. The statement read “Growth in sales makes me more comfortable about cash, not less” and awarded full marks for agreeing — when believing that is precisely the misconception Week 1 exists to correct. It now reads: “I understand why a fast-growing month can leave less cash in the bank, not more.”

R3

Scoring 4 out of 20 by answering “No” throughout

No change needed

This one is working as intended. Two statements are deliberately negative — “We have delayed a supplier payment to manage cash” and “A tax, VAT or annual payment has caught us out.” Answering “No” to those is good news, so each earns two marks. Four out of twenty is the diagnostic working, not failing.

R4

Negatively worded questions needed systematic testing

Fixed

We audited all ten statements; question 3 was the only one scored the wrong way. We then changed how questions are defined so the scoring direction must be stated explicitly for every one — the site now refuses to build if anyone adds a question without declaring which answer is the good one. Results of the full test are overleaf.

R5

Buttons promised payment, the page offered the waitlist

Fixed

Not a wording problem. The buttons carried fixed text while the enrolment page separately checked whether card payment was switched on. Both now read from the same fact. While payment is off, every primary button reads “Join the founding-cohort waitlist” and the enrolment page is headed “Enrolment opens shortly”. The day payment goes live they change back on their own, with nothing for anyone to remember to edit.

R6

“The same Excel model Carl uses” overclaimed

Fixed

Now reads “The Excel model Carl builds with fractional CFO clients”, and the templates section says they are built from the models you use. That reflects what you told us: different models for different industries.

R7

Confidentiality answered with an absolute “Yes”

Condition applies

The flat “Yes” is gone. The answer now says you choose what you share, that every participant accepts a confidentiality obligation in the course terms, and points to the privacy policy for how information is held. We have deliberately said nothing about recordings, because you flagged that policy as still to be agreed. The condition: that answer now refers to an obligation in the course terms, and those terms are not yet written. It must not go live in front of customers until they are.

Why “Yes” to everything does not score full marks

Answering “Yes” to every statement includes agreeing that you have delayed a supplier payment to manage cash, and that a tax bill has caught you out in the last year. Those are not signs of good cash control, so the score stops at 16. Only the genuinely ideal set of answers reaches 20.

05

Emails to your customers

5 items needed

The site already captures diagnostic results, waitlist sign-ups and enrolments, storing the consent wording and timestamp with each, so the list is compliant from day one. What it cannot yet do is send anything. There are two jobs here, and they need different tools.

Two jobs, two tools

Automatic emails are sent by the website without you: receipts, welcome and joining instructions, session reminders, refund confirmations. They must arrive within seconds and must never be filtered as marketing.

Broadcasts are sent by you, when you choose: cohort announcements, the waitlist nurture sequence, anything to the diagnostic list. You need a screen you can write and send from yourself, without us.

To set both up, we need the following from you.

  • The sending addressFor example hello@cashflowmastery.co, or your existing carl@independentadvisors.ai. Also the name it should appear from, and where replies should go.
  • The domain, and access to its DNS settingsConfirmation of who owns cashflowmastery.co, with the registrar login or an introduction to whoever holds it. We need to add three records proving the mail is genuinely from you. Without them your emails land in spam — the most common reason course emails are never seen. This is the one item here we cannot work around.
  • Which provider, and whose account paysWe recommend Resend for automatic emails (free to 3,000 a month) and MailerLite for broadcasts (free to 1,000 subscribers). Both can sit on your account, so the list stays yours.
  • A postal address for the footer of marketing emailsLegally expected in most markets and required by both providers.
  • Who writes the emailsWe build and connect them. We need wording for the welcome, joining instructions and reminders — or your approval to draft them in your voice for your review.
